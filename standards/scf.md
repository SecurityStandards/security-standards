# Secure Controls Framework (v2020.4)

Referência oficial: https://www.securecontrolsframework.com/download-scf

## Controles e Domínios (32)

### Security & Privacy Governance

- **GOV-01**: Mechanisms exist to facilitate the implementation of cybersecurity and privacy governance controls.
- **GOV-02**: Mechanisms exist to establish, maintain and disseminate cybersecurity and privacy policies, standards and procedures.
- **GOV-03**: Mechanisms exist to review the cybersecurity and privacy program, including policies, standards and procedures, at planned intervals or if significant changes occur to ensure their continuing suitability, adequacy and effectiveness.
- **GOV-04**: Mechanisms exist to assign a qualified individual with the mission and resources to centrally-manage, coordinate, develop, implement and maintain an enterprise-wide cybersecurity and privacy program.
- **GOV-05**: Mechanisms exist to develop, report and monitor cybersecurity and privacy program measures of performance.
- **GOV-05.1**: Mechanisms exist to develop, report and monitor Key Performance Indicators (KPIs) to assist organizational management in performance monitoring and trend analysis of the cybersecurity and privacy program.
- **GOV-05.2**: Mechanisms exist to develop, report and monitor Key Risk Indicators (KRIs) to assist senior management in performance monitoring and trend analysis of the cybersecurity and privacy program.
- **GOV-06**: Mechanisms exist to identify and document appropriate contacts within relevant law enforcement and regulatory bodies.
- **GOV-07**: Mechanisms exist to establish contact with selected groups and associations within the cybersecurity & privacy communities to: 
 ▪ Facilitate ongoing cybersecurity and privacy education and training for organizational personnel;
 ▪ Maintain currency with recommended cybersecurity and privacy practices, techniques and technologies; and
 ▪ Share current security-related information including threats, vulnerabilities and incidents.
- **GOV-08**: Mechanisms exist to define the context of its business model and document the mission of the organization.
- **GOV-09**: Mechanisms exist to establish control objectives as the basis for the selection, implementation and management of the organization’s internal control system.

### Asset Management

- **AST-01**: Mechanisms exist to facilitate an IT Asset Management (ITAM) program to implement and manage asset management controls.
- **AST-01.1**: Mechanisms exist to identify and assess the security of technology assets that support more than one critical business function.
- **AST-01.2**: Mechanisms exist to identify and involve pertinent stakeholders of critical systems, applications and services to support the ongoing secure management of those assets.
- **AST-02**: Mechanisms exist to inventory system components that:
 ▪ Accurately reflects the current system; 
 ▪ Is at the level of granularity deemed necessary for tracking and reporting;
 ▪ Includes organization-defined information deemed necessary to achieve effective property accountability; and
 ▪ Is available for review and audit by designated organizational officials.

- **AST-02.1**: Mechanisms exist to update asset inventories as part of component installations, removals and asset upgrades.
- **AST-02.2**: Automated mechanisms exist to detect and respond to the presence of unauthorized hardware, software and firmware components.
- **AST-02.3**: Mechanisms exist to establish and maintain an authoritative source and repository to provide a trusted source and accountability for approved and implemented system components that prevents assets from being duplicated in other asset inventories
- **AST-02.4**: Mechanisms exist to track instances of approved deviations from the standardized baseline configuration.
- **AST-02.5**: Automated mechanisms exist to employ Network Access Control (NAC) that is capable of detecting unauthorized devices and disable network access to those unauthorized devices.
- **AST-02.6**: Mechanisms exist to enable Dynamic Host Configuration Protocol (DHCP) server logging to improve asset inventories and assist in detecting unknown systems.
- **AST-02.7**: Mechanisms exist to ensure compliance with software licensing restrictions.
- **AST-02.8**: Mechanisms exist to create and maintain a map of systems of where sensitive data is stored, transmitted or processed.
- **AST-02.9**: Mechanisms exist to implement and manage a Configuration Management Database (CMDB), or similar technology, to collect and manage asset-specific information.
- **AST-03**: Mechanisms exist to assign asset ownership responsibilities to a department, team or individual that establishes a common understanding of controls to protect assets.
- **AST-03.1**: Mechanisms exist to include capturing the name, position and/or role of individuals responsible/accountable for administering assets as part of the asset inventory process.
- **AST-04**: Mechanisms exist to maintain network architecture diagrams that: 
 ▪ Contain sufficient detail to assess the security of the network's architecture;
 ▪ Reflect the current state of the network environment; and
 ▪ Document all sensitive data flows.
- **AST-05**: Mechanisms exist to maintain strict control over the internal or external distribution of any kind of sensitive media.
- **AST-06**: Mechanisms exist to implement enhanced protection measures for unattended systems to protect against tampering and unauthorized access.
- **AST-06.1**: Mechanisms exist to educate users on the need to physically secure laptops and other mobile devices out of site when traveling, preferably in the trunk of a vehicle.
- **AST-07**: Mechanisms exist to protect devices that capture sensitive data via direct physical interaction from tampering and substitution.
- **AST-08**: Mechanisms exist to inspect mobile devices for evidence of tampering upon return from geographic regions of concern or other known hostile environments that could lead to device compromise.
- **AST-09**: Mechanisms exist to securely destroy media when it is no longer needed for business or legal reasons.
- **AST-10**: Mechanisms exist to ensure that employees and third-party users return all organizational assets in their possession upon termination of employment, contract or agreement.
- **AST-11**: Mechanisms exist to authorize, control and track systems entering and exiting organizational facilities.
- **AST-12**: Mechanisms exist to restrict the possession and usage of personally-owned technology devices within organization-controlled facilities.
- **AST-13**: Mechanisms exist to reduce the risk associated with third-party assets that are attached to the network from harming organizational assets or exfiltrating organizational data.
- **AST-14**: Mechanisms exist to enforce and monitor usage parameters that limit the potential damage caused from the unauthorized alteration of system parameters. 

- **AST-15**: Mechanisms exist to validate the integrity of configuration settings of critical systems, system components or services throughout all phases of the Secure Development Life Cycle (SDLC).
- **AST-15.1**: Mechanisms exist to physically and logically inspect critical systems to detect evidence of tampering.
- **AST-16**: Mechanisms exist to implement and govern a Bring Your Own Device (BYOD) program to reduce risk associated with personally-owned devices in the workplace.

### Business Continuity & Disaster Recovery

- **BCD-01**: Mechanisms exist to facilitate the implementation of contingency planning controls to help ensure resilient assets and services.
- **BCD-01.1**: Mechanisms exist to coordinate contingency plan development with internal and external elements responsible for related plans.
- **BCD-01.2**: Mechanisms exist to coordinate internal contingency plans with the contingency plans of external service providers to ensure that contingency controls can be satisfied.
- **BCD-01.3**: Mechanisms exist to redeploy personnel to other roles during a disruptive event or in the execution of a continuity plan.
- **BCD-01.4**: Mechanisms exist to configure the alternate storage site to facilitate recovery operations in accordance with Recovery Time Objectives (RTOs) and Recovery Point Objectives (RPOs).
- **BCD-02**: Mechanisms exist to identify and document the critical systems, applications and services that support essential missions and business functions.
- **BCD-02.1**: Mechanisms exist to resume all missions and business functions within Recovery Time Objectives (RTOs) of the contingency plan's activation.
- **BCD-02.2**: Mechanisms exist to continue essential missions and business functions with little or no loss of operational continuity and sustain that continuity until full system restoration at primary processing and/or storage sites.
- **BCD-02.3**: Mechanisms exist to resume essential missions and business functions within an organization-defined time period of contingency plan activation.
- **BCD-03**: Mechanisms exist to train applicable contingency personnel in their contingency roles and responsibilities.
- **BCD-03.1**: Mechanisms exist to incorporate simulated events into contingency training to facilitate effective response by personnel in crisis situations.
- **BCD-03.2**: Automated mechanisms exist to provide a more thorough and realistic contingency training environment.
- **BCD-04**: Mechanisms exist to conduct tests and/or exercises to determine the contingency plan's effectiveness and the organization’s readiness to execute the plan.
- **BCD-04.1**: Mechanisms exist to coordinate contingency plan testing with internal and external elements responsible for related plans.
- **BCD-04.2**: Mechanisms exist to test the contingency plan at the alternate processing site to both familiarize contingency personnel with the facility and evaluate the capabilities of the alternate processing site to support contingency operations.
- **BCD-05**: Mechanisms exist to conduct a Root Cause Analysis (RCA) and "lessons learned" activity every time the contingency plan is activated.
- **BCD-06**: Mechanisms exist to keep contingency plans current with business needs and technology changes.
- **BCD-07**: Mechanisms exist to implement alternative or compensating controls to satisfy security functions when the primary means of implementing the security function is unavailable or compromised.
- **BCD-08**: Mechanisms exist to establish an alternate storage site that includes both the assets and necessary agreements to permit the storage and recovery of system backup information.
- **BCD-08.1**: Mechanisms exist to separate the alternate storage site from the primary storage site to reduce susceptibility to similar threats.
- **BCD-08.2**: Mechanisms exist to identify and mitigate potential accessibility problems to the alternate storage site in the event of an area-wide disruption or disaster.
- **BCD-09**: Mechanisms exist to establish an alternate processing site that provides security measures equivalent to that of the primary site.
- **BCD-09.1**: Mechanisms exist to separate the alternate processing site from the primary processing site to reduce susceptibility to similar threats.
- **BCD-09.2**: Mechanisms exist to identify potential accessibility problems to the alternate processing site and possible mitigation actions, in the event of an area-wide disruption or disaster.
- **BCD-09.3**: Mechanisms exist to address priority-of-service provisions in alternate processing and storage sites that support availability controls, including Recovery Time Objectives (RTOs).
- **BCD-09.4**: Mechanisms exist to prepare the alternate processing site so that the site is ready to be used as the operational site; capable of supporting essential missions and business functions.
- **BCD-09.5**: Mechanisms exist to plan and prepare for both natural and manmade circumstances that preclude returning to the primary processing site.
- **BCD-10**: Mechanisms exist to reduce the likelihood of a single point of failure with primary telecommunications services.
- **BCD-10.1**: Mechanisms exist to formalize primary and alternate telecommunications service agreements to contain priority-of-service provisions that support availability controls, including Recovery Time Objectives (RTOs).
- **BCD-10.2**: Mechanisms exist to obtain alternate telecommunications services from providers that are separated from primary service providers to reduce susceptibility to the same threats.
- **BCD-10.3**: Mechanisms exist to contractually-require telecommunications service providers to have contingency plans that meet organizational contingency controls.
- **BCD-11**: Mechanisms exist to create recurring backups of data, software and system images to ensure the availability of the data.
- **BCD-11.1**: Mechanisms exist to routinely test backups that verifies the reliability of the backup process, as well as the integrity and availability of the data.
- **BCD-11.2**: Mechanisms exist to store backup copies of critical software and other security-related information in a separate facility or in a fire-rated container that is not collocated with the system being backed up.
- **BCD-11.3**: Mechanisms exist to reimage assets from configuration-controlled and integrity-protected images that represent a secure, operational state.
- **BCD-11.4**: Cryptographic mechanisms exist to prevent the unauthorized disclosure and modification of backup information.
- **BCD-11.5**: Mechanisms exist to utilize sampling of available backups to test recovery capabilities as part of contingency plan testing.
- **BCD-11.6**: Mechanisms exist to transfer information system backup data to the alternate storage site at a rate that is capable of meeting Recovery Time Objectives (RTOs) and Recovery Point Objectives (RPOs).
- **BCD-11.7**: Mechanisms exist to maintain a failover system that is not collocated with the primary system and that can be activated without loss of information or disruption to operations.
- **BCD-11.8**: Mechanisms exist to implement and enforce dual authorization for the deletion or destruction of sensitive backup media and data.
- **BCD-12**: Mechanisms exist to ensure the recovery and reconstitution of systems to a known state after a disruption, compromise or failure.
- **BCD-12.1**: Mechanisms exist to utilize specialized backup mechanisms that will allow transaction recovery for transaction-based applications and services in accordance with Recovery Point Objectives (RPOs).
- **BCD-12.2**: Mechanisms exist to implement real-time or near-real-time failover capability to maintain availability of critical systems.
- **BCD-12.3**: Mechanisms exist to utilize electronic discovery (eDiscovery) that covers current and archived communication transactions.
- **BCD-12.4**: Mechanisms exist to restore information system components within organization-defined restoration time-periods from configuration-controlled and integrity-protected information; representing a known, operational state for the components.
- **BCD-13**: Mechanisms exist to protect backup and restoration hardware and software.

### Capacity & Performance Planning

- **CAP-01**: Mechanisms exist to facilitate the implementation of capacity management controls to ensure optimal system performance for future capacity controls.
- **CAP-02**: Mechanisms exist to control resource utilization of systems that are susceptible to Denial of Service (DoS) attacks to limit and prioritize the use of resources.
- **CAP-03**: Mechanisms exist to conducted capacity planning so that necessary capacity for information processing, telecommunications and environmental support will exist during contingency operations.

### Change Management

- **CHG-01**: Mechanisms exist to facilitate the implementation of change management controls.
- **CHG-02**: Mechanisms exist to govern the technical configuration change control processes.
- **CHG-02.1**: Mechanisms exist to prohibit unauthorized changes, unless designated approvals are received.
- **CHG-02.2**: Mechanisms exist to test and document proposed changes in a non-production environment before changes are implemented in a production environment.
- **CHG-02.3**: Mechanisms exist to include a cybersecurity representative in the configuration change control review process.
- **CHG-02.4**: Automated mechanisms exist to implement remediation actions upon the detection of unauthorized baseline configurations change(s).
- **CHG-02.5**: Mechanisms exist to govern assets involved in providing cryptographic protections according to the organization's configuration management processes.
- **CHG-03**: Mechanisms exist to analyze proposed changes for potential security impacts, prior to the implementation of the change.
- **CHG-04**: Mechanisms exist to enforce configuration restrictions in an effort to restrict the ability of users to conduct unauthorized changes.
- **CHG-04.1**: Mechanisms exist to perform after-the-fact reviews of configuration change logs to discover any unauthorized changes.
- **CHG-04.2**: Mechanisms exist to prevent the installation of software and firmware components without verification that the component has been digitally signed using a certificate that is recognized and approved by the organization.
- **CHG-04.3**: Mechanisms exist to enforce a two-person rule for implementing changes to critical assets.
- **CHG-04.4**: Mechanisms exist to limit operational privileges for implementing changes.
- **CHG-04.5**: Mechanisms exist to restrict software library privileges to those individuals with a pertinent business need for access.
- **CHG-05**: Mechanisms exist to ensure stakeholders are made aware of and understand the impact of proposed changes.
- **CHG-06**: Mechanisms exist to verify the functionality of security controls when anomalies are discovered.
- **CHG-06.1**: Mechanisms exist to report the results of security and privacy function verification to senior management.

### Cloud Security

- **CLD-01**: Mechanisms exist to facilitate the implementation of cloud management controls to ensure cloud instances are secure and in-line with industry practices.
- **CLD-02**: Mechanisms exist to ensure the cloud security architecture supports the organization's technology strategy to securely design, configure and maintain cloud employments.
- **CLD-03**: Mechanisms exist to host security-specific technologies in a dedicated subnet.
- **CLD-04**: Mechanisms exist to ensure support for secure interoperability between components.
- **CLD-05**: Mechanisms exist to ensure the integrity of virtual machine images at all times.
- **CLD-06**: Mechanisms exist to ensure multi-tenant owned or managed assets (physical and virtual) are designed and governed such that provider and customer (tenant) user access is appropriately segmented from other tenant users.
- **CLD-07**: Mechanisms exist to ensure cloud providers use secure protocols for the import, export and management of data in cloud-based services.
- **CLD-08**: Mechanisms exist to ensure interoperability by requiring cloud providers to use industry-recognized formats and provide documentation of custom changes for review.
- **CLD-09**: Mechanisms exist to control the location of cloud processing/storage based on business controls, as well as statutory, regulatory and contractual obligations.
- **CLD-10**: Mechanisms exist to limit and manage the storage of sensitive data in public cloud providers.
- **CLD-11**: Mechanisms exist to utilize Cloud Access Points (CAPs) to provide boundary protection and monitoring functions that both provide access to the cloud and protect the organization from the cloud.

### Compliance

- **CPL-01**: Mechanisms exist to facilitate the identification and implementation of relevant legislative statutory, regulatory and contractual controls.
- **CPL-02**: Mechanisms exist to provide a security controls oversight function.
- **CPL-02.1**: Mechanisms exist to implement an internal audit function that is capable of providing senior organization management with insights into the appropriateness of the organization's technology and information governance processes.
- **CPL-03**: Mechanisms exist to ensure managers regularly review the processes and documented procedures within their area of responsibility to adhere to appropriate security policies, standards and other applicable controls.
- **CPL-03.1**: Mechanisms exist to utilize independent assessors at planned intervals or when the system, service or project undergoes significant changes.
- **CPL-03.2**: Mechanisms exist to regularly review assets for compliance with the organization’s cybersecurity and privacy policies and standards.
- **CPL-04**: Mechanisms exist to plan audits that minimize the impact of audit activities on business operations.

### Configuration Management

- **CFG-01**: Mechanisms exist to facilitate the implementation of configuration management controls.
- **CFG-01.1**: Mechanisms exist to implement a segregation of duties for configuration management that prevents developers from performing production configuration management duties.
- **CFG-02**: Mechanisms exist to develop, document and maintain secure baseline configurations for technology platform that are consistent with industry-accepted system hardening standards.
- **CFG-02.1**: Mechanisms exist to review and update baseline configurations:
 ▪ At least annually;
 ▪ When required due to so; or
 ▪ As part of system component installations and upgrades.
- **CFG-02.2**: Automated mechanisms exist to govern and report on baseline configurations of the systems.
- **CFG-02.3**: Mechanisms exist to retain previous versions of baseline configuration to support roll back.
- **CFG-02.4**: Mechanisms exist to manage baseline configurations for development and test environments separately from operational baseline configurations to minimize the risk of accidental changes.
- **CFG-02.5**: Mechanisms exist to configure systems utilized in high-risk areas with more restrictive baseline configurations.
- **CFG-02.6**: Mechanisms exist to configure network devices to synchronize startup and running configuration files.
- **CFG-02.7**: Mechanisms exist to document and manage approved deviations to standardized configurations.
- **CFG-02.8**: Mechanisms exist to respond to unauthorized changes to configuration settings as security incidents.
- **CFG-02.9**: Mechanisms exist to allow baseline controls to be specialized or customized by applying a defined set of tailoring actions that are specific to:
 ▪ Mission / business functions;
 ▪ Operational environment;
 ▪ Specific threats or vulnerabilities; or
 ▪ Other conditions or situations that could affect mission / business success.
- **CFG-03**: Mechanisms exist to configure systems to provide only essential capabilities by specifically prohibiting or restricting the use of ports, protocols, and/or services.
- **CFG-03.1**: Mechanisms exist to periodically review system configurations to identify and disable unnecessary and/or non-secure functions, ports, protocols and services.
- **CFG-03.2**: Automated mechanisms exist to prevent the execution of unauthorized software programs.
- **CFG-03.3**: Mechanisms exist to whitelist or blacklist applications in an order to limit what is authorized to execute on systems.
- **CFG-03.4**: Mechanisms exist to prevent systems from creating split tunneling connections or similar techniques that could be used to exfiltrate data.
- **CFG-04**: Mechanisms exist to enforce software usage restrictions to comply with applicable contract agreements and copyright laws.
- **CFG-04.1**: Mechanisms exist to establish parameters for the secure use of open source software.
- **CFG-04.2**: Mechanisms exist to allow only approved Internet browsers and email clients to run on systems.
- **CFG-05**: Mechanisms exist to restrict the ability of non-privileged users to install unauthorized software.
- **CFG-05.1**: Mechanisms exist to configure systems to generate an alert when the unauthorized installation of software is detected.
- **CFG-05.2**: Mechanisms exist to prohibit the installation of software, unless the action is performed by a privileged user or service.

### Monitoring

- **MON-01**: Mechanisms exist to facilitate the implementation of enterprise-wide monitoring controls.
- **MON-01.1**: Mechanisms exist to implement Intrusion Detection / Prevention Systems (IDS / IPS) technologies on critical systems, key network segments and network choke points.
- **MON-01.2**: Mechanisms exist to utilize a Security Incident Event Manager (SIEM) or similar automated tool, to support near real-time analysis and the escalation of events.
- **MON-01.3**: Mechanisms exist to continuously monitor inbound and outbound communications traffic for unusual or unauthorized activities or conditions.
- **MON-01.4**: Mechanisms exist to monitor, correlate and respond to alerts from physical, cybersecurity, privacy and supply chain activities to achieve integrated situational awareness.
- **MON-01.5**: Mechanisms exist to utilize Wireless Intrusion Detection / Protection Systems (WIDS / WIPS) to identify rogue wireless devices and to detect attack attempts via wireless networks.
- **MON-01.6**: Mechanisms exist to utilize Host-based Intrusion Detection / Prevention Systems (HIDS/HIPS) to actively alert on or block unwanted activities and send logs to a Security Incident Event Manager (SIEM), or similar automated tool, to maintain situational awareness.
- **MON-01.7**: Mechanisms exist to utilize a File Integrity Monitor (FIM) or similar change-detection technology on critical assets to generate alerts for unauthorized modifications.
- **MON-01.8**: Mechanisms exist to review event logs on an ongoing basis and escalate incidents in accordance with established timelines and procedures.
- **MON-01.9**: Mechanisms exist to log all Internet-bound requests, in order to identify prohibited activities and assist incident handlers with identifying potentially compromised systems.
- **MON-01.10**: Mechanisms exist to monitor deactivated accounts for attempted usage.
- **MON-01.11**: Automated mechanisms exist to alert incident response personnel of detected suspicious events and implement actions to terminate suspicious events.
- **MON-01.12**: Automated mechanisms exist to alert incident response personnel of inappropriate or unusual activities that have security incident implications.
- **MON-01.13**: Mechanisms exist to "tune" event monitoring technologies through analyzing communications traffic/event patterns and developing profiles representing common traffic patterns and/or events.
- **MON-01.14**: Mechanisms exist to implement enhanced activity monitoring for individuals who have been identified as posing an increased level of risk.
- **MON-01.15**: Mechanisms exist to implement enhanced activity monitoring for privileged users.
- **MON-01.16**: Mechanisms exist to assess the organization's needs for monitoring and prioritize the monitoring of assets, based on asset criticality and the sensitivity of the data it stores, transmits and processes.
- **MON-02**: Mechanisms exist to utilize a Security Incident Event Manager (SIEM) or similar automated tool, to support the centralized collection of security-related event logs.
- **MON-02.1**: Automated mechanisms exist to correlate logs from across the enterprise by a Security Incident Event Manager (SIEM) or similar automated tool, to maintain situational awareness.
- **MON-02.2**: Automated mechanisms exist to centrally collect, review and analyze audit records from multiple sources.
- **MON-02.3**: Automated mechanisms exist to integrate the analysis of audit records with analysis of vulnerability scanners, network performance, system monitoring and other sources to further enhance the ability to identify inappropriate or unusual activity.
- **MON-02.4**: Automated mechanisms exist to correlate information from audit records with information obtained from monitoring physical access to further enhance the ability to identify suspicious, inappropriate, unusual or malevolent activity.
- **MON-02.5**: Mechanisms exist to specify the permitted actions for both users and systems associated with the review, analysis and reporting of audit information.
- **MON-02.6**: Mechanisms exist to adjust the level of audit review, analysis and reporting based on evolving threat information from law enforcement, industry associations or other credible sources of threat intelligence.
- **MON-02.7**: Automated mechanisms exist to compile audit records into an organization-wide audit trail that is time-correlated.
- **MON-02.8**: Mechanisms exist to provide privileged users or roles the capability to change the auditing to be performed on specified information system components based on specific event criteria within specified time thresholds.
- **MON-03**: Mechanisms exist to configure systems to produce audit records that contain sufficient information to, at a minimum:
 ▪ Establish what type of event occurred;
 ▪ When (date and time) the event occurred;
 ▪ Where the event occurred;
 ▪ The source of the event;
 ▪ The outcome (success or failure) of the event; and 
 ▪ The identity of any user/subject associated with the event.
- **MON-03.1**: Mechanisms exist to protect sensitive data contained in log files.
- **MON-03.2**: Mechanisms exist to link system access to individual users or service accounts.
- **MON-03.3**: Mechanisms exist to log and review the actions of users and/or services with elevated privileges.
- **MON-03.4**: Mechanisms exist to verbosely log all traffic (both allowed and blocked) arriving at network boundary devices, including firewalls, Intrusion Detection / Prevention Systems (IDS/IPS) and inbound and outbound proxies.
- **MON-03.5**: Mechanisms exist to limit Personal Data (PD) contained in audit records to the elements identified in the privacy risk assessment.
- **MON-03.6**: Mechanisms exist to centrally manage and configure the content required to be captured in audit records generated by organization-defined information system components.
- **MON-04**: Mechanisms exist to allocate and proactively manage sufficient audit record storage capacity to reduce the likelihood of such capacity being exceeded.
- **MON-05**: Mechanisms exist to alert appropriate personnel in the event of a log processing failure and take actions to remedy the incident.
- **MON-05.1**: Mechanisms exist to provide 24x7x365 near real-time alerting capability when a log processing failure occurs.
- **MON-05.2**: Automated mechanisms exist to alert appropriate personnel or roles when the allocated volume reaches an organization-defined percentage of maximum audit record storage capacity.
- **MON-06**: Mechanisms exist to provide an event log report generation capability to aid in detecting and assessing anomalous activities.
- **MON-06.1**: Mechanisms exist to provide and implement the capability for auditing the parameters of user query events for data sets containing Personal Data (PD).
- **MON-06.2**: Mechanisms exist to employ trend analyses to determine if security control implementations, the frequency of continuous monitoring activities, and/or the types of activities used in the continuous monitoring process need to be modified based on empirical data.
- **MON-07**: Mechanisms exist to configure systems to use internal system clocks to generate time stamps for audit records.
- **MON-07.1**: Mechanisms exist to synchronize internal system clocks with an authoritative time source.
- **MON-08**: Mechanisms exist to protect event logs and audit tools from unauthorized access, modification and deletion.
- **MON-08.1**: Mechanisms exist to back up audit records onto a physically different system or system component than the Security Incident Event Manager (SIEM) or similar automated tool.
- **MON-08.2**: Mechanisms exist to restrict access to the management of event logs to privileged users with a specific business need.
- **MON-08.3**: Cryptographic mechanisms exist to protect the integrity of audit information and audit tools.
- **MON-08.4**: Automated mechanisms exist to enforce dual authorization for the movement or deletion of audit information.
- **MON-09**: Mechanisms exist to utilize a non-repudiation capability to protect against an individual falsely denying having performed a particular action.
- **MON-10**: Mechanisms exist to retain audit records for a time period consistent with records retention controls to provide support for after-the-fact investigations of security incidents and to meet statutory, regulatory and contractual retention controls.
- **MON-11**: Mechanisms exist to monitor for evidence of unauthorized exfiltration or disclosure of non-public information.
- **MON-11.1**: Automated mechanisms exist to analyze network traffic to detect covert data exfiltration.
- **MON-11.2**: Automated mechanisms exist to detect unauthorized network services and alert incident response personnel.
- **MON-11.3**: Automated mechanisms exist to identify and alert on Indicators of Compromise (IoC).
- **MON-12**: Mechanisms exist to provide session audit capabilities that: 
 ▪ Capture and log all content related to a user session; and
 ▪ Remotely view all content related to an established user session in real time.
- **MON-13**: Mechanisms exist to provide an alternate audit capability in the event of a failure in primary audit capability.
- **MON-14**: Mechanisms exist to coordinate sanitized audit information among external organizations to identify anomalous events when audit information is shared across organizational boundaries, without giving away sensitive or critical business data.
- **MON-14.1**: Mechanisms exist to share audit information with third-party organizations based on specific cross-organizational sharing agreements.
- **MON-15**: Mechanisms exist to conduct covert channel analysis to identify aspects of communications that are potential avenues for covert channels.
- **MON-16**: Mechanisms exist to detect and respond to anomalous behavior that could indicate account compromise or other malicious activities.
- **MON-16.1**: Mechanisms exist to monitor internal personnel activity for potential security incidents.
- **MON-16.2**: Mechanisms exist to monitor third-party personnel activity for potential security incidents.
- **MON-16.3**: Mechanisms exist to monitor for unauthorized activities, accounts, connections, devices and software.

### Cryptographic Protections

- **CRY-01**: Mechanisms exist to facilitate the implementation of cryptographic protections controls using known public standards and trusted cryptographic technologies.
- **CRY-01.1**: Cryptographic mechanisms exist to prevent unauthorized disclosure of information as an alternate to physical safeguards.
- **CRY-01.2**: Mechanisms exist to address the exporting of cryptographic technologies in compliance with relevant statutory and regulatory controls.
- **CRY-01.3**: Cryptographic mechanisms exist to ensure the confidentiality and integrity of information during preparation for transmission and during reception.
- **CRY-01.4**: Cryptographic mechanisms exist to conceal or randomize communication patterns.
- **CRY-02**: Automated mechanisms exist to enable systems to authenticate to a cryptographic module.
- **CRY-03**: Cryptographic mechanisms exist to protect the confidentiality of data being transmitted.
- **CRY-04**: Cryptographic mechanisms exist to protect the integrity of data being transmitted.
- **CRY-05**: Cryptographic mechanisms exist to prevent unauthorized disclosure of information at rest.
- **CRY-05.1**: Cryptographic mechanisms exist to protect the confidentiality and integrity of the information being stored on storage media.
- **CRY-05.2**: Mechanisms exist to remove unused data from online storage and archive it off-line in a secure location until it can be disposed of according to data retention controls.
- **CRY-06**: Cryptographic mechanisms exist to protect the confidentiality and integrity of non-console administrative access.
- **CRY-07**: Mechanisms exist to protect wireless access via secure authentication and encryption.
- **CRY-08**: Mechanisms exist to implement an internal Public Key Infrastructure (PKI) infrastructure or obtain PKI services from a reputable PKI service provider.
- **CRY-08.1**: Resiliency mechanisms exist to ensure the availability of data in the event of the loss of cryptographic keys.
- **CRY-09**: Mechanisms exist to facilitate cryptographic key management controls to protect the confidentiality, integrity and availability of keys.
- **CRY-09.1**: Mechanisms exist to facilitate the production and management of symmetric cryptographic keys using Federal Information Processing Standards (FIPS)-compliant key management technology and processes.
- **CRY-09.2**: Mechanisms exist to facilitate the production and management of asymmetric cryptographic keys using approved key management technology and processes that protect the user’s private key.
- **CRY-09.3**: Mechanisms exist to ensure the availability of information in the event of the loss of cryptographic keys by individual users.
- **CRY-09.4**: Mechanisms exist to facilitate the secure distribution of symmetric and asymmetric cryptographic keys using industry recognized key management technology and processes.
- **CRY-09.5**: Mechanisms exist to ensure cryptographic keys are bound to individual identities.
- **CRY-10**: Mechanisms exist to ensure systems associate security attributes with information exchanged between systems.

### Data Classification & Handling

- **DCH-01**: Mechanisms exist to facilitate the implementation of data protection controls.
- **DCH-01.1**: Mechanisms exist to ensure data stewardship is assigned, documented and communicated.
- **DCH-02**: Mechanisms exist to ensure data and assets are categorized in accordance with applicable statutory, regulatory and contractual controls.
